My Krishna,

I've started this letter more times than I want to admit. Every version sounded too small for what I actually feel, so I'm going to stop trying to make it perfect and just tell you the truth.

This is the first of your birthdays that I get to be a part of. We met six months ago, and for a while you were just someone I knew. I didn't see it coming, the way you slowly became the person I think about first and last every day. Today I can't imagine a single morning without your “good morning” being the first thing I look for. I don't know how someone rearranges an entire life this quietly, but you did. And I wouldn't undo one second of it.

Do you remember the 14th of September? You asked me, “Bhul gya na mujhe?” and when I told you I love you, you wrote:

“Lag nhi rha wese. But believe kr hi leti hoon.”

I have thought about that line more than you will ever know. You chose to believe me before I had earned it. That is the bravest, kindest thing anyone has ever done for me. And since that day, everything I do is me trying to make it lag. I hope, by now, it does, at least a little. And if it doesn't yet, that's okay. I'm not going anywhere until it does.

You grew up in Assam. I grew up in Kashmir. Two people from opposite corners of the same country, who both had to fly thousands of kilometres to a city neither of us knew, just to find each other. I don't believe in many things, Krishna. But I believe in that.

I know birthdays far from home can feel a little quiet. Your family is far away today, and if some part of you misses home right now, that's okay. Let it. I can't be the people who have loved you your whole life, or the house you grew up in, or the food that tastes like your childhood. But I can promise you this: as long as I'm here, you will never be alone in this country. Not on your birthday. Not on a random Tuesday. Not at 2 a.m. when everything feels heavy and you don't want to say it out loud.

Mere hotay huwe tumhe kya hoga. I said it once as a filmy dialogue. Today I'm saying it as a vow.

I love your eyes, which honestly are unfair to the rest of the world. I love that you laugh at your own jokes before you even finish them. I love how much you care about people, even the ones who never notice. I love your 623 question marks and your 193 eye-rolls. I even love you when you make my blood boil, maybe especially then, because even when we fight, you are still the one person I want to make things right with.

You don't always see yourself the way I see you. So today, just for today, borrow my eyes. You are kind. You are so much stronger than you think. You are braver than you give yourself credit for. And you are loved, completely, by someone who has no plans of ever stopping.

Thank you for being born. Thank you to your family, for raising the person I love. And thank you, Krishna, for letting me in, for every “??????”, for every good night, for choosing to believe me when you didn't have to.

I once told you that loving you was the only promise I intend to keep in my life. I'm writing it down again here, so you can hold me to it forever: I will keep choosing you. On the easy days and the hard ones. In Karlsruhe, or in Kashmir, or in Assam, or wherever this life decides to take us.

Happy birthday, my Chamak challo. This is the first one.
I want to be there for every single one after it.

Forever yours,
Arman 💛

P.S. If you cried, you're allowed to blame it on the cake. If you didn't, I'll try harder next year. 😁
